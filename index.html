Postdoctoral Scholar · Stanford Radiation Oncology

I make hidden biological structure visible.

I build interpretable AI that turns biomedical tables, networks, and transcriptomes into spatial maps—so models can learn the relationships and researchers can inspect them.

My work connects computational genomics, computer vision, and explainable AI, with current applications in liquid biopsy, multimodal cancer analysis, graph learning, and single-cell biology.

Open research

From difficult data to inspectable maps.

Three connected projects explore the same central idea at different biological scales: representation is not a formatting detail—it can be the mechanism that makes learning and interpretation possible.

Lead work · Biomedical tabular AI

Dynomap

Learns where features belong while learning the prediction task, converting unordered clinical and molecular tables into continuous spatial maps.

Up to 18%higher multiclass cancer-subtype accuracy in liquid-biopsy experiments
Visit project ↗ Preprint
Lead work · Biological networks

Graph2Image

Transforms very large biological graphs into image sets, giving vision models global context while making node-level evidence directly visible.

>1 billionnodes can be analyzed on a personal computer in reported experiments
Visit project ↗ Preprint
Collaborative work · Single-cell biology

scVision

Places co-expressed genes near one another, renders each cell as an image, and learns a reusable representation through masked-image pretraining.

72 millionhuman cells used for self-supervised pretraining
